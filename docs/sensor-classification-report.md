# Synthetic sensor-classification benchmark: stopped at sensory feasibility

** result:** The prospectively fixed initial sensor representation failed its first neural-information gate. No teacher or student was trained; no DN message, five-arm comparison, or confirmation run occurred. This is a new, isolated synthetic-task feasibility result and does not revise the successful or failed RPS findings.

## Task and pathway

The [locked protocol](sensor-classification-protocol.md) defines balanced, overlapping synthetic normal, overheating, and excessive-vibration distributions for two continuous readings. Temperature drives existing VPN lane 0 at 40–190 Hz; vibration drives lane 1 independently at 40–190 Hz. Both are present together. The generating class, sample position, intended response, and outcome do not enter the neural drive. The real-wiring spiking network produces the same three group-MBON rates used by the existing engineered contextual bandit. KC→MBON multipliers were fixed at one. If the sensory gate had passed, the existing teacher-preference code would have used measured DN3141/3165 spikes and a decoded-action imitation update; this downstream mechanism was **not exercised** for this benchmark.

## Locked first gate and observed measurements

Before any probe, the gate required a calibration-only nearest-centroid rule on three standardized `log1p` MBON rates to score at least **26/36 overall** and **7/12 for each class** on independent held-out examples. The same threshold on the raw two-sensor comparator checked dataset learnability. Calibration had 72 balanced examples, evaluation 36 balanced examples, with separate data seeds and separate calibration/student neural seeds. All probes were reward-free; one keyed probe repeated exactly. At least two MBON components had to vary, with finite rates and unchanged neural weights.

| Held-out measure | Normal | Overheating | Excess vibration | Total | Gate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Two raw sensor values, analytical centroid | 10/12 | 12/12 | 10/12 | **32/36** | Pass |
| Three simulated MBON rates, analytical centroid | 9/12 | 7/12 | **2/12** | **18/36** | **Fail** |

MBON confusion counts (true row → predicted normal / overheating / vibration) were normal **9/2/1**, overheating **3/7/2**, and vibration **4/6/2**. Mean held-out MBON group rates were normal **0.387/0.161/0.298**, overheating **0.875/0.506/0.726**, and vibration **0.738/0.369/0.583** spikes per neuron. All three components varied; the keyed replay matched exactly; neural multipliers stayed one. These averages show that vibration examples tended to produce intermediate group activity in this fixed encoding, but they do not prove which synapses or stimulation interaction caused the overlap. The label-blind analytic rule is an information check, not a controller installed in either fly. Chance for balanced three-class guessing is 12/36.

The raw readings retain useful class information, but this fixed graded two-VPN encoding and three-MBON feature population did not preserve enough for the predeclared gate, especially for excessive vibration. The low neural score cannot be attributed to teacher-message interference because no DN pulse was sent. It also does not test whether another classifier could recover information from these features; the one specified fixed decoder failed. Under the prospective stop rule, no alternative encoding, feature selection, decoder, stimulation rate, or seed was tried.

## Read-only layer and decoder audit

We reconstructed all **36** held-out predictions directly from each saved standardized feature vector and the **unchanged calibration centroid**: squared Euclidean distances and lowest-index tie handling reproduced every archived prediction. The resulting totals remained raw **32/36** (normal/overheating/vibration **10/12, 12/12, 10/12**) and pooled MBON **18/36** (**9/12, 7/12, 2/12**). Of the ten vibration examples recognized from raw readings, **eight** were misclassified after the pooled-MBON measurement and fixed decoder. Across all classes, 15 raw-correct examples became MBON-decoder errors; one raw-decoder error became MBON-correct. This recount changes no earlier gate.

| Stage | Saved per probe? | Supported observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw temperature/vibration and latent analysis label | **Yes** | Continuous values are present; the fixed raw analytical decoder separates 32/36. Labels are used for scoring, not stimulation. |
| Requested VPN drive | **Yes** | The two requested rates are saved. Temperature drives lane 0, vibration lane 1, each on **19 distinct VPN indices** (`laneVpn[0/1].slice(2)`); no target overlaps and lane 2 is off. This establishes independent *assignment of drive*, not independent or distinct actual VPN firing. |
| Actual VPN spike counts | **No** | The simulator has per-neuron `net.count`, but the runner did not save the VPN counts or spike identities. Actual lane response, saturation, and cross-lane interaction cannot be assessed. |
| KC population activity | **No** | No KC counts or active identities were retained; it is unknown whether vibration remains distinct after VPN→KC propagation. |
| Individual MBON counts | **No** | The simulator counts individual MBON spikes, but the runner saved only the mean of each **14-cell** action group. Any distinction erased by averaging cannot be tested from this archive. |
| Three pooled MBON rates and fixed decisions | **Yes** | Rates, `log1p`-standardized features, calibration centroids, all three distances, and predictions are saved for every calibration and held-out probe. |

The held-out class means of the three pooled rates (spikes/cell, normal **0.387/0.161/0.298**, overheating **0.875/0.506/0.726**, vibration **0.738/0.369/0.583**) put vibration between normal and overheating on **all three** coordinates. The calibration centroids have the same coordinate ordering. Vibration was called normal **4/12**, overheating **6/12**, and vibration **2/12**. This is observed overlap under the fixed three-rate summary and nearest-centroid boundary. It does **not** show that all vibration information is absent from the pooled rates, or that a different boundary would pass; no other decoder was fit or tested. In particular, the archive cannot distinguish loss at VPN firing, KC propagation, individual MBON activity, 14-cell pooling, or limitations of the fixed centroid rule.

The code path confirms the fixed stimulation and timing: `tools/check_sensor_classification.mjs` uses `40+3×(temperature−35)` and `40+15×vibration` Hz, resets the full neural state before **each** probe, replaces the neural RNG with a keyed stream, resets spike counts at **50 ms**, and reads the three MBON group means after stepping through **0–239 ms**. Calibration uses dataset seed **610001**, probe seed **610002**, network constructor seeds **12030/12032**, and separate keyed probe streams. The constructor seeds do not independently affect these real-wiring probe spikes: `sense()` replaces `net.rand` before every probe and real wiring is seed-invariant. Thus the effective neural randomness comes from the distinct keyed streams, while the physical network and initial state are the same. There were no teacher messages or rewards, and the weight-integrity check passed.

**Decision: A, deferred until after HackPrinceton.** A single prospectively locked, frozen population-recording diagnostic on predetermined sensor inputs could test whether the elevated vibration drive yields a distinct VPN pattern, whether that survives into KCs and individual MBONs, and whether averaging 14 MBONs obscures it. It should predefine recordings and comparisons before collecting new spikes, without trying new encoding rates or fitting multiple decoders. This is a specific, testable missing measurement; the current archive cannot justify a sensory correction. With the event imminent and a validated RPS demonstration available, further sensor work is **not** justified before Princeton.
