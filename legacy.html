What the experiments actually showed

An optional contextual-bandit readout learned all three RPS actions from game rewards in four saved students (65, 65, 63, 65/72 held-out wins); its stricter all-arm paired comparison failed for one student. KC→MBON synaptic weights were frozen. Contextual-bandit experiment

The cue-specific DNge175 teaching pilots demonstrated real message delivery, immediate action changes, own-reward updates, and disconnected student performance. The locked trained-teacher-specific retained-learning gates failed. Shuffled, untrained, or generic vetoes could also produce strong policies; one generic control failed its matching gate. The Challenge replay above shows those recorded trials. Teaching research story

An external analytical decoder classified 98/108 held-out RPS neural input states from three MBON rates. This decoder is not the fly's action policy. RPS information analysis

The experimental hybrid readout won 50/72, 50/72, and 54/72 held-out rounds in the original locked comparison. Reliable three-action learning failed its predefined gates. Hybrid experiment

The later experimental nonlinear RL controller used simulated MBON rates, a small additional network trained from RPS rewards, and frozen KC→MBON weights. Individual students 681 and 683 passed the three-action criteria; student 682 failed, so the locked all-student success criterion failed. This does not show reliable three-action learning by the original dopamine-only fly. Nonlinear experiment

At 180 ms of each round, the channel reads three intermediate neurons downstream of the learning synapses. It quantizes each spike count to 0, 1 or 2 and drives two reserved student visual neurons per value until 240 ms. The pulse above appears only when a recorded nonzero signal is delivered. These source and target cells are engineering choices, not a known biological communication pathway.
