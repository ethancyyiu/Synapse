Did compact advice actually help?

We trained three identical local classifiers on account-disjoint synthetic scenarios. Only supervision changed: limited true labels alone, extra compact risk messages from a deterministic teacher, or the same messages shuffled. The teacher was absent for all 120 held-out decisions.

Separate real Gemini check ? improvement gate failed

After the rule-fixture study, we locked a new 12-message Gemini Flash-Lite training run on synthetic training cases only. On the untouched 120-case test, the Gemini-guided local student matched the independent baseline: 25/36 suspicious cases caught, zero false alerts, precision 1.000, F1 0.820. The result does not show a benefit from Gemini supervision. Locked protocol ? Separate result.

Synapse began as a study of knowledge transfer between two simulated neural systems. Controlled RPS tasks showed compact teacher information could accelerate a student's independent learning. Synapse Fraud explores the same teacher–student principle in financial risk detection using an engineered classifier; the fraud model does not use fly neurons.

Prototype boundaries: the account data is mock, labels are generated from declared scenarios, model scores are uncalibrated, and review decisions only change this browser session. A frontier teacher can be configured for a separate on-demand comparison or optional local-student training; neither was called in the recorded experiment.

Separate real Gemini experiment · synthetic labels

Does the teacher beat matched controls?

We locked new account and scenario-family splits, trained three seven-feature local students, and tested on 48 separate synthetic cases. The limited-label baseline, an arm with 12 additional independent labels, and an arm with 12 structured Gemini messages used the same features. No Gemini call was made for inference.
